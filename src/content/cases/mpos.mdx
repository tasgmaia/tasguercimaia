<Section label="Overview" title="Brazil has around 45 million people, without any bank account or any access to financial services producing 817bi reais in products and services">

In Brazil millions of people, in the working-age, are lacking a formal work position. Therefore they are either dragged into the informal market or become entrepreneurs by necessity. In this scenario, many companies started to look at these people and began to develop accessible financial services for them.

By the time some solutions began to emerge, Stone Co. saw a great opportunity to act. The main business advantage, compared to our competitors, was that we had an operation based on personal relationships in small and medium cities. And we designed a singular solution that had no predecessor in the Brazilian market.

<Section>

When we built this app, there was only one player in Brazil in this specific market. Then we did a deep benchmark to understand how the service worked. We analyzed three companies on this benchmark. The first was the single-player in Brazil at this point. The two other were international players that targeted the same kind of audience. Also, we conducted interviews with users that were familiar with our competitor's current solution.

We had two crucial outputs of this phase. First, we found a strategic direction for the product, second, we designed a diagram that helped the team organize the service in many areas, such as customer service.

</Section>

<Section id="strategy" label="Product strategic modeling" />

<Quote source="Main competitor's user quote">“I don't understand why I have to use two apps to operate my business”</Quote>

<Section>

The most important decision on the strategic and architectural side was that we would have a single app. This decision came in opposition to the competitor's solution where users had to download two apps, one for transactions and the other to manage. The two apps' solution was by far one of the main pain points of users, on the other hand, it was a quick win to differentiate and quickly build a significant user base.

Therefore, during the Information Architecture process, we managed to design three main flows, identification, selling and managing. Each one of these flows with different intensity of use. For example, the identification flow was intended to be used just while creating an account or login in. The selling flow was intended to be the most used, so it worked as the main flow, and the managing was designed to be a secondary flow.

</Section>
